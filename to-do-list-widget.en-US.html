To-do list widget for iPhone: how to set it up so you actually use it

A to-do list widget only helps if the right list is on it, at a size you can read, on a screen you look at. This guide walks through those three choices with Mochi, a cute to-do list for iPhone with a Home Screen widget and an Apple Watch app. Feature details are taken from Mochi’s App Store listing and Mochi’s own widget guide; where the listing is silent, we say so.

1. Pick the list the widget shows

The widget is a window, not a filing cabinet. Give it one short list that changes every day, and keep everything else in a second list inside the app.

If your day looks like…Put this list on the widget
Work or schoolToday: the 3–4 tasks that have to happen before you log off
Busy householdHome: one chore, one errand, one bill, one thing for you
Building a routineEvery day: the few habits you want in front of you each day

Four tasks is the free limit per list, and it is also about what a widget can show you in one glance. Give each task an emoji (📚, 🧺, 💡, 🏃) so you can read the widget before you read the words.

2. Pick a size you can read in one second

Mochi’s App Store listing says “Home Screen widget” but does not list sizes. According to Mochi’s widget guide, you can choose small, medium or large on iPhone (iPad also has extra large), and larger sizes show more tasks at once. A simple rule of thumb:

  • Small: a reminder that the list exists. Good next to your most-used apps.
  • Medium: one row of the Home Screen. A good default for a short daily list.
  • Large: half a screen. Worth it if this is the screen you open first every morning.

The widget uses the same Mochi theme you picked in the app, so choose a theme that reads well against your wallpaper (5 themes are free; Premium unlocks all 101).

3. Pick the spot you look at most

  • First Home Screen page, top row. You see it every time you unlock your phone.
  • In a stack. iOS lets you stack widgets of the same size and swipe between them, so the list can share a spot with your calendar or weather widget.
  • On iPad. If you plan at a desk, a larger widget on the iPad Home Screen gives the list room to breathe.

Add the widget in under a minute

  1. Install Mochi, open it once and create the list you want on the widget.
  2. Touch and hold an empty spot on the Home Screen until the icons jiggle, then tap Edit (or +) and Add Widget.
  3. Search for Mochi, swipe to the size you want and tap Add Widget, then Done.
  4. If the widget shows old or sample tasks, open Mochi once so it can refresh, or remove the widget and add it again.

The App Store listing does not say which plan the widget needs, so check in the app. Mochi’s App Store listing requires iOS 17 or later.

Widget plus reminders. A widget works when you look at your phone; a reminder works when you don’t. Mochi’s App Store listing includes reminders, so give the one task with a real deadline a reminder and let the widget hold the rest of the list. Your todo list widget stays calm and cute instead of turning into an alarm.

When your phone is in a bag, the same list is on Apple Watch: the App Store listing says you can keep your list “on a Home Screen widget and Apple Watch.” Mochi’s widget guide covers adding it to a watch face.

Frequently asked questions

Does Mochi have a to-do list widget for iPhone?

Yes. Mochi’s App Store listing says you can keep your list on a Home Screen widget and on Apple Watch.

Which widget sizes can I choose?

The App Store listing does not list sizes. Mochi’s own widget guide on this site lists small, medium and large on iPhone (iPad also has extra large); larger sizes show more tasks at once.

Is there a Lock Screen widget?

The App Store listing mentions a Home Screen widget and Apple Watch. It does not mention a Lock Screen widget, so this page does not promise one.

Can I check off tasks from the widget?

The App Store listing does not say. In the app, you tap the circle next to a task when it is done.

Does the widget match my theme?

Mochi’s widget guide says the widget uses the same Mochi theme you picked in the app. There are 101 themes (5 free, 96 with Premium).

What can I do with the free version?

The free version includes 2 lists with up to 4 tasks each, 5 themes and 1 emoji set. Premium, an in-app purchase, unlocks unlimited lists and tasks, all 101 themes and all 11 emoji sets.
