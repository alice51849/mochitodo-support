When Mochi is the right pick (and when it isn’t)

Short answer: Mochi is a good pick if you want a small, cute checklist on your iPhone where every task can get its own emoji, and you like glancing at it on a Home Screen widget or your Apple Watch. It’s not the one to get if you need shared lists, plain-English date entry or a focus timer, since its App Store listing doesn’t mention those. And if you want more than 2 lists of 4 tasks without paying, know that Premium is an in-app purchase.

You’ll probably like Mochi if…

Your lists are short and personal: today’s errands, a grocery run, homework for the week, a morning routine. Mochi’s whole idea is that checking things off should feel nice, so each task can wear a little emoji and you can switch between 101 themes (5 are free). The listing also mentions reminders, a daily planner view and habit tracking, so you don’t need a second app just for routines.

It also makes sense if you mostly look at your list rather than open it. The widget and the Apple Watch app are there for exactly that.

Pick something else if…

  • You plan with someone else. Mochi’s listing says nothing about shared lists. MinimaList (below) does describe shared lists.
  • You like typing “dentist next Tuesday 3pm” and having the app figure it out. Tend and MinimaList both describe that kind of input. Mochi’s listing doesn’t.
  • You want a notebook more than a checklist. If you write long notes with fonts and formatting, mimi is built around that.
  • You want a lot of lists for free. Mochi’s free version is 2 lists with up to 4 tasks each. That’s enough to try it out, but a heavy list-maker will hit the limit quickly.
  • You need iPad or Mac. The listing talks about iPhone, widgets and Apple Watch; it doesn’t mention iPad or Mac, so check the store page before counting on it.

FAQ

When is Mochi the right to-do list app?

When you want a short, cute checklist on your iPhone where each task can have its own emoji, and you like seeing it on a Home Screen widget or your Apple Watch. It covers school, work, errands, groceries, habits and daily routines in one place.

Who should pick something else?

People who need to share a list with someone else, type dates in plain English, use a focus timer, or keep long formatted notes. Mochi’s App Store listing doesn’t mention any of those. Also, if you want lots of lists without paying, the free version stops at 2 lists of up to 4 tasks each.

What’s free and what costs extra?

The free version includes 2 lists with up to 4 tasks each, 5 themes and 1 emoji set. Premium is an in-app purchase that unlocks unlimited lists and tasks, all 101 themes and all 11 emoji sets.

Does Mochi remind me about tasks?

Yes. The listing says it has reminders that keep you on track.

Is Mochi on Android?

Yes, Mochi is also on Google Play.
