Daily ADHD checklist and morning routine

A daily checklist that works for ADHD is short, visible, and in the order you actually do things. Start with a five-to-seven-step morning routine, one “launch pad” check before leaving, three priorities for the day, and a two-minute evening reset. Copy the template below and cut it down.

Why the list should be short

Long to-do lists tend to become wallpaper: you stop seeing them. Many people with ADHD say a list works better when it is short enough to read in one glance, ordered the way the morning really unfolds, and placed where they look anyway — the lock screen, the bathroom mirror, the front door. The template below is deliberately compact. Treat it as a menu: keep the steps that fit your life, delete the rest, and do not add more until the short version feels automatic.

  • One action per line. “Take meds with water” is clearer than “health stuff”.
  • Start with a verb. Fill, pack, put, check.
  • Same order every day. Routine reduces decisions.
  • Celebrate the tick. Checking something off is the reward; make it satisfying.

Morning routine checklist

  • ☐ Drink a glass of water (put the glass out the night before)
  • ☐ Take any daily medication as prescribed
  • ☐ Bathroom: teeth, face, deodorant
  • ☐ Get dressed in the outfit chosen last night
  • ☐ Eat something with protein, even small
  • ☐ Look at today’s three priorities
  • ☐ Launch pad check (see below), then leave

If seven steps feel like a lot, start with three: water, dressed, launch pad. Add one step a week.

Launch pad: before you leave the house

A launch pad is one fixed spot by the door where everything you need to leave lives. Instead of remembering ten things, you remember one place.

  • ☐ Keys
  • ☐ Phone and charger
  • ☐ Wallet or cards
  • ☐ Bag packed for today (laptop, books, gym clothes)
  • ☐ Anything that must go out: letters, returns, lunch from the fridge

Daytime: three priorities

Pick at most three tasks that would make today count. Write them as concrete first steps, not projects: “open the tax form and fill page one” instead of “do taxes”.

  • ☐ Priority 1: ______
  • ☐ Priority 2: ______
  • ☐ Priority 3: ______
  • ☐ One small kind thing for future you (refill water, reply to one message)

Helpful habits many people use: set a timer and work in short blocks with a break in between; body doubling (working alongside someone, in person or on a video call); and putting the very next step where you will see it instead of in your head.

Evening reset (about 10 minutes)

  • ☐ Return keys, wallet and phone charger to the launch pad
  • ☐ Lay out tomorrow’s clothes
  • ☐ Put a glass by the sink or bed for the morning water
  • ☐ Clear one surface: desk, counter, or nightstand
  • ☐ Write tomorrow’s three priorities
  • ☐ Phone on the charger, away from the bed if you can

Bad day? Do the shortest version: keys to the launch pad, phone on the charger. That still counts.

Making the checklist stick

  1. Make it visible. Out of sight is out of mind; a widget on the Home Screen or a note on the mirror beats a list buried in an app.
  2. Use a reminder for the one step you skip most. Not for every step — alert fatigue is real.
  3. Make it pleasant. A list you like looking at is a list you open. Colors, themes and cute details are not silly if they get you to look.
  4. Review weekly. Delete steps you never do and move a step that always gets skipped to a different time.

Frequently asked questions

What should be on a daily ADHD checklist?

Keep it short: a few morning steps in real order, a launch pad check before leaving, three priorities, and a short evening reset. Fewer, clearer steps are easier to keep.

How many steps should a morning routine have?

Start with three to five and add one at a time once the short version is automatic.

Is this checklist a treatment for ADHD?

No. It is a general organization habit, not medical advice. For diagnosis and treatment, talk to a doctor.
