New Year Goals Checklist 2027: A Reset You Can Actually Keep

Short answer: a New Year reset works better as a short checklist than as a long list of resolutions. Spend one evening looking back at 2026, clear out what you are carrying over, pick no more than three goals for 2027, and turn each one into a small action you can tick off this week. Then keep only the next four things in front of you. If your 2026 list was gone by February, the problem was probably not willpower: the list was too long and you stopped seeing it. Below is the full reset checklist to copy, a version split into two lists of four, a plan for restarting if you quit around the second week of January, and how to keep it in Mochi: Cute Checklist on your iPhone.

The New Year reset checklist (copy this)

Do these in order, ideally between December 26 and January 3. Each block takes about 20–30 minutes.

1. Look back at 2026

  • ☐ Write three things that went well this year
  • ☐ Write one goal you dropped, and the week you stopped
  • ☐ Note what got in the way: time, energy, or simply forgetting

2. Clear what you are carrying over

  • ☐ Empty or archive old to-do lists you no longer open
  • ☐ Cancel or finish three small leftover tasks
  • ☐ Tidy your phone's Home Screen so the apps you want to use are easy to reach

3. Choose your 2027 goals

  • ☐ Pick at most three areas, for example health, money and learning
  • ☐ Write one goal per area in a single sentence
  • ☐ Turn each goal into a weekly action small enough to do on a tired day

4. Set up the first week

  • ☐ Put the first four actions on one short list
  • ☐ Add a reminder for anything tied to a time
  • ☐ Choose a fixed restart day, such as every Monday

The short version: two lists of four

A full checklist is good for planning, but daily you only need to see what matters now. These two lists fit the free version of Mochi exactly: two lists with four tasks each.

List “2027 goals this week”

  1. Move for 15 minutes, three times
  2. Move a set amount to savings
  3. Read 10 pages a day
  4. Sleep before midnight on weeknights

List “Reset tasks”

  1. Archive old lists
  2. Finish three leftover tasks
  3. Tidy the Home Screen
  4. Pick your restart day

Quit in the second week of January? How to start again

Many people stop around the second Friday of January (January 8 in 2027), a date often called “Quitter's Day.” Stopping is normal. What matters is how quickly you come back:

  1. Do not start from zero. Keep every task you already ticked off. A week done is still a week done.
  2. Shrink the goal. “Work out every day” becomes “put on shoes and walk for 10 minutes.” Make it small enough that skipping feels silly.
  3. Cut to one list of four. If two lists felt like too much, keep only the four actions you most want, and park the rest in a note.
  4. Use your restart day. Pick the next Monday, set a reminder for the morning, and tick the first circle that day.
  5. Keep it in sight. A list you see every time you unlock your phone is easier to return to than one hidden in a notebook.

Frequently asked questions

How many New Year goals should I set?

Three or fewer is easier to keep. Write one sentence per goal and turn each into a small weekly action.

What should a New Year reset checklist include?

A short look back at the past year, clearing leftover tasks and old lists, choosing a few goals, and setting up the first week with reminders and a fixed restart day.

What if I already gave up on my resolutions?

Shrink the goal, keep only four actions on one list, pick the next Monday as your restart day and tick the first one that day. You do not need to wait for next year.
