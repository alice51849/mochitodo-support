Revision timetable for GCSEs and A-levels: a template you can stick to

Short answer: a revision timetable works when it is built backwards from your exam dates, splits each subject into small topics, and leaves you only three or four revision tasks a day. How to build a revision timetable in five steps

  1. List your exams. Write every paper with its date. Your school or exam board timetable is the source; check it rather than guessing.
  2. Break subjects into topics. Use your specification or the topic list from your teacher. “Biology” is not a task; “Cell division: flashcards” is.
  3. Work backwards. Earlier papers get topics first. Leave the last week before each exam for past papers and weak spots.
  4. Keep sessions short. Many students use 25 to 45 minute blocks with a break. Three or four blocks a day on school days is realistic; more on weekends and study leave.
  5. Plan rest on purpose. One evening off a week and proper sleep keep the timetable going until the last paper.

Daily revision checklist (copy into your phone)

Today’s revision

  • ☐ 📚 Block 1: one topic, notes into flashcards
  • ☐ ✏️ Block 2: ten practice questions
  • ☐ 🔄 Review yesterday’s flashcards
  • ☐ 👀 Write tomorrow’s three topics

Four items is enough. Finish them and stop; a timetable that leaves you exhausted on Monday is gone by Thursday.

Frequently asked questions

When should I start my GCSE or A-level revision timetable?

Many students start a light timetable a few months before exams and add more blocks closer to them. What matters most is that every topic gets covered before its paper, so start with your exam dates and work backwards.

How many hours a day should I revise?

There is no single right number. On school days, three or four focused blocks of 25 to 45 minutes are realistic for many students; on weekends and study leave you can add more, with breaks.

Should I revise one subject a day or mix them?

Mixing two or three subjects a day keeps every subject moving and makes the timetable easier to keep. Put past papers in the final weeks.
