Moving checklist for Canada: an 8-week timeline

Moving in Canada has a few twists a generic checklist misses: leases that all seem to end on the same day, a federal and a provincial address change, and moving days that can mean snow, ice and a frozen truck ramp. The safest plan is still to work backward from moving day: book help 8 weeks out, handle address changes and utilities 4 weeks out, pack room by room in the last two weeks, and keep a first-night box with you. Copy the timeline below and put your own dates next to each heading.

How to use this checklist

Write your moving date at the top, then count back and date each heading. If you have less than eight weeks, start at the heading that matches the time you have and clear the earlier sections quickly. Two Canadian timing traps are worth marking first:

  • Month-end and July 1. Movers and rental trucks book up at the end of every month, and in Quebec, where many leases end on June 30, July 1 is famously the busiest moving day of the year. If your date falls there, book help first and everything else second.
  • Your tenancy notice. If you rent, the notice you must give your landlord is set by your province’s residential tenancy rules and your lease. Check it before you sign for the new place, not after.

Then copy the full list, delete what does not apply, and each week pull that week’s tasks into a short list you look at every day. Keep one running list of “people and services to notify”; it always grows.

8 to 6 weeks before

  • ☐ Set a moving budget and decide: professional movers, rental truck, or friends with a borrowed van
  • ☐ Get at least two written quotes and compare what each includes (packing, stairs, long carries, insurance)
  • ☐ Book your mover or truck as soon as you choose, especially for month-end, summer weekends or July 1
  • ☐ Give notice to your landlord as your province and lease require, and get it acknowledged in writing
  • ☐ Walk through every room and sort into keep, sell, donate and recycle
  • ☐ Start using up frozen food and pantry items
  • ☐ Create a moving folder for the lease, quotes, receipts and an inventory of valuable items
  • ☐ Measure large furniture and the doorways, stairwells and elevator at the new place

4 weeks before

  • ☐ Gather boxes, tape, markers, bubble wrap and a few wardrobe boxes
  • ☐ Set up Canada Post Mail Forwarding so letters follow you while you update everyone
  • ☐ Update your address with the Canada Revenue Agency (through My Account or by phone) so tax and benefit mail reaches you
  • ☐ Update your bank, credit cards, employer, insurance, phone plan, subscriptions and online stores
  • ☐ Book power (hydro), gas and water shut-off at the old place and start-up at the new one
  • ☐ Book internet installation early; technicians can be booked up for weeks
  • ☐ Move or start tenant insurance or home insurance so it covers the new address from day one
  • ☐ Transfer school, daycare and medical records if needed
  • ☐ Book time off work for moving day and the day after

2 weeks before

  • ☐ Pack room by room, labelling each box with the room and a short contents list
  • ☐ Confirm date, time and both addresses with movers or helpers
  • ☐ Book the service elevator and loading dock at both buildings, and ask about any deposit or time window
  • ☐ Return borrowed items, library books and anything at the dry cleaner or repair shop
  • ☐ Refill prescriptions so you are not hunting for a pharmacy in week one
  • ☐ Plan meals that use up what is left in the fridge and freezer

Moving in winter: extra items for snow and cold

  • ☐ Shovel and salt the walkway, steps and driveway at both places the night before and again in the morning
  • ☐ Lay down floor runners or old blankets at both entrances so slush stays off the floors
  • ☐ Keep a shovel, ice scraper and a bag of salt or sand in your own car
  • ☐ Move electronics, plants, paint, cleaning products and anything liquid in your heated car, not the truck
  • ☐ Let electronics warm up to room temperature before you switch them on
  • ☐ Check the forecast two days ahead and agree on a backup date with your movers
  • ☐ Make sure the heat is on at the new place before the first box arrives

Moving day

  • ☐ Pack the first-night box and carry it yourself: chargers, toiletries, towels, sheets, medicine, snacks, toilet paper, basic tools
  • ☐ Carry documents, keys, passports, valuables and your laptop yourself
  • ☐ Final walk-through of every room, closet, cupboard and drawer
  • ☐ Take photos of the old place’s condition and the meter readings
  • ☐ Check the truck inventory against your list before it leaves
  • ☐ At the new place: note any damage and the meter readings, then direct boxes to the right rooms
  • ☐ Make the beds before you do anything else

First weeks after the move

  • ☐ Update the address on your driver’s licence and vehicle registration within the time your province sets
  • ☐ Update your provincial health card address
  • ☐ If you moved to another province: apply for the new province’s health coverage as soon as you arrive, and check how long your old coverage continues
  • ☐ If you moved to another province: exchange your driver’s licence and register your vehicle within the new province’s deadline
  • ☐ Update your voter registration with Elections Canada and your provincial elections office
  • ☐ Join a family doctor registry or waitlist if you moved to a new city
  • ☐ Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms and find the breaker panel and main water shut-off
  • ☐ Break down boxes and recycle packing material as your new municipality sorts it

Frequently asked questions

When should I start my moving checklist?

About eight weeks ahead is comfortable. If you are moving at month-end, in summer or around July 1 in Quebec, book movers or a truck even earlier, then follow the rest of the timeline in order.

Who do I need to notify when I move in Canada?

Canada Post (mail forwarding), the Canada Revenue Agency, your bank and credit cards, employer, insurance, utilities, internet and phone providers, subscriptions, schools and doctors, plus your province for your driver’s licence, vehicle registration and health card.

What changes if I move to another province?

You will usually need the new province’s health coverage, driver’s licence and vehicle registration, each with its own deadline, and health coverage rules and any waiting period vary by province. Check the official sites of both provinces before you go.
