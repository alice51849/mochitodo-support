New baby checklist

Get ready for a new baby in four parts: pack a hospital bag around week 35–36, set up a safe sleep space and feeding basics, have an installed car seat for going home, and keep a short list for the first weeks (appointments, paperwork, help from friends). Copy the lists below and cut what you do not need.

How to use this new baby checklist

Babies need less stuff than registries suggest, but the few essentials matter a lot. This list is split by when you need things: the hospital bag, the home setup, going home, and the first weeks. Ask your hospital or birth center for their own list as well — many supply diapers, wipes and some postpartum items, so you can skip those.

  1. Copy the lists and share them with your partner or helper.
  2. Mark items you already have, then items someone can lend you.
  3. Pack the hospital bag a few weeks early and leave it by the door.
  4. Keep the first-weeks list short and visible; you will be tired.

Hospital bag

For the birthing parent

  • ☐ ID, insurance card and hospital paperwork
  • ☐ Birth preferences, if you wrote any
  • ☐ Phone, long charging cable and portable battery
  • ☐ Comfortable robe, slippers and warm socks
  • ☐ Loose going-home outfit
  • ☐ Nursing bras or comfortable bras and nursing pads
  • ☐ Toiletries, lip balm, hair ties, glasses or contacts
  • ☐ High-waisted underwear and maternity pads (ask what the hospital provides)
  • ☐ Snacks and a water bottle with a straw

For the baby

  • ☐ Going-home outfit in newborn and 0–3 month sizes
  • ☐ Hat and socks or booties
  • ☐ Swaddle or light blanket
  • ☐ Rear-facing infant car seat, already installed in the car

For the partner or support person

  • ☐ Change of clothes, toiletries, snacks
  • ☐ Phone charger and a list of people to message

Home essentials

Sleep

  • ☐ Crib, bassinet or play yard that meets current safety standards
  • ☐ Firm, flat mattress with a fitted sheet only — no pillows, bumpers or loose blankets
  • ☐ Two or three spare fitted sheets
  • ☐ Sleep sacks or swaddles

Feeding

  • ☐ If breastfeeding: nursing pillow, nipple cream, pump if needed, milk storage bags
  • ☐ If formula or bottle feeding: bottles, nipples, formula your pediatrician recommends, bottle brush
  • ☐ Burp cloths (more than you think)

Diapering and bath

  • ☐ Newborn diapers and a few size 1 packs
  • ☐ Wipes and diaper cream
  • ☐ Changing pad and a trash can with a lid
  • ☐ Baby bath tub or sink insert, mild baby wash, soft towels
  • ☐ Nail clippers or file

Clothes and health

  • ☐ Bodysuits and sleepers that zip or snap down the front
  • ☐ Baby thermometer
  • ☐ Nasal aspirator
  • ☐ Pediatrician’s phone number saved in your phone

Going home

  • ☐ Car seat installed correctly — many areas offer free checks by certified technicians
  • ☐ Baby’s going-home outfit suited to the weather
  • ☐ Discharge papers and instructions
  • ☐ First pediatrician appointment booked
  • ☐ Birth registration paperwork started as your local rules require

The first weeks at home

  • ☐ Pediatrician visits and any follow-up appointments for the birthing parent
  • ☐ Add the baby to your health insurance within the required window
  • ☐ Apply for birth certificate and other documents as your local rules require
  • ☐ Stock easy meals; accept every offer of food
  • ☐ Write down who is helping with what: meals, laundry, older siblings, pets
  • ☐ Track feedings and diapers if your pediatrician asks you to
  • ☐ Sleep when you can and ask for help

Frequently asked questions

When should I pack my hospital bag?

Many people pack around weeks 35–36 so it is ready early. Ask your hospital what they provide so you do not pack duplicates.

What do I really need for a newborn?

A safe sleep space, a rear-facing car seat, feeding supplies, diapers and wipes, a few outfits, and burp cloths. Most other gear can wait until you know what you need.

Is this list medical advice?

No. It is a general preparation list. Follow your doctor, midwife or pediatrician on health, feeding and sleep questions.
