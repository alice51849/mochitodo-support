How to set up a study planner for Year 12

  1. Write down every due date. School assessment tasks, SACs or internal assessments, trial exams and final exams. Your school calendar and your state’s official exam timetable are the sources.
  2. Split subjects into tasks. “Chemistry” is not a task; “Equilibrium: 10 practice questions” is.
  3. Work backwards from each date. The next assessment gets priority; keep one practice exam block per subject each week as finals get closer.
  4. Fit study around your week. Mark shifts, training and family commitments first, then place study blocks in the gaps.
  5. Protect rest. One free evening a week and enough sleep keep the planner realistic through Term 3 and 4.

Frequently asked questions

When should I start an HSC or VCE study planner?

As early in Year 12 as you can, even with a light plan. Start with your assessment and exam dates, then add blocks as each one gets closer.

How long should I study each day in Year 12?

It depends on your subjects and commitments. Three or four focused blocks on school days is realistic for many students, with more on weekends and in the holidays before exams.

How do I fit study around a part-time job?

Put shifts and training into your week first, then place study blocks in the gaps, and keep a short review task for busy days so the habit does not break.
