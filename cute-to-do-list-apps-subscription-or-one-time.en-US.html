Cute to-do list apps: subscription or one-time purchase?

Cute and aesthetic to-do list apps are almost all free to download. The difference is what happens when you want more: some ask for a monthly or yearly subscription, some sell a one-time unlock, and some offer both. Here is how four popular ones charge on the US App Store, and when our one-time-purchase app Mochi is, and is not, the better fit.

Mochi is our app. We describe the other apps using only what their public App Store pages say, and we list our own weak spots too. Checked on November 26, 2026 on the US App Store (apps that came up for “cute checklist” and “aesthetic to do list”). Prices, offers and ratings change; check the current listing before you buy. App Store pages show up to 10 purchase items, so a list may not be complete.

Over a year

  • mimi and Zinnia: a subscription keeps renewing each month or year until you cancel it.
  • Smile Todo “forever” and MinimaList “Lifetime”: you pay once.
  • Mochi Premium: you pay once on the App Store.

So a one-time purchase is not unique to Mochi: Smile Todo and MinimaList also sell one. On the US App Store, Smile Todo’s “forever” unlock costs the same as Mochi Premium, and MinimaList’s cheapest “Lifetime” option costs a little more. If you only need an app for a month or two, a low-cost monthly plan, like mimi’s, can cost less than a one-time unlock, as long as you remember to cancel it.

Where Mochi is a good fit

  • You pay once, or not at all. The free version stays free. Premium is one purchase on the App Store, with nothing to cancel later.
  • Cute without the clutter. Give each task its own little emoji and tap the circle when it’s done. Choose from 101 themes on iPhone (5 free, 96 with Premium) and 11 emoji sets with Premium.
  • Reminders for the tasks you don’t want to forget. The Google Play listing also describes repeating reminders and a record of finished items.
  • On iPhone: a Home Screen widget and Apple Watch.
  • No subscription on either store. The Google Play listing also states there are no ads.

Where Mochi is not the right choice

  • The free version is small. It gives you 2 lists with up to 4 tasks each (plus 5 themes and 1 emoji set on iPhone). That’s enough to see if you like it, but not enough for most people’s daily lists. Unlimited lists and tasks need Premium.
  • It’s a checklist first. If you want a sticker journal or a planner with page layouts, apps built around that, like mimi or Zinnia, will suit you better.
  • Few ratings, and a lower average. Mochi has 59 US ratings averaging 4.2 stars, so you have less to go on than with the apps above.
  • No cross-platform sync is described. Our store listings don’t mention syncing lists between iPhone and Android, or a web version. If you need that, choose an app that lists it.
  • Separate stores, separate purchases. Buying on the App Store doesn’t unlock the Android app, and the Google Play price may differ from the App Store price.

A quick way to check any app

  1. Before you start entering your tasks, open the app’s App Store page and scroll to In-App Purchases. Words like “weekly”, “monthly” or “yearly” usually mean a subscription. Words like “forever” or “lifetime” usually mean you pay once.
  2. Check how many lists or tasks the free version allows.
  3. Read a few of the newest 1–3 star reviews, not just the average rating.
  4. If you subscribe, note the renewal date. You can cancel in your Apple ID’s Subscriptions settings.
