Winter Break Chore Chart for Kids: A List You Can Copy

Short answer: keep a winter break chore chart short and the same every day. Pick two or three daily chores per child that match their age, add one bigger “helper job” each week, and put the list where everyone can see it. Most U.S. schools are off for about two weeks around late December and early January (check your district calendar for exact dates). Below are chores by age with boxes you can copy, a daily rhythm, a four-task version per child and how to keep it in Mochi: Cute Checklist on your iPhone so you are not the only one remembering who does what.

Chores by age

These are common starting points. Every child is different, so choose what fits your family and show the job once before adding it to the chart.

Ages 3–5

  • ☐ Put toys back in their bin
  • ☐ Put dirty clothes in the hamper
  • ☐ Help set napkins and spoons on the table
  • ☐ Feed a pet with a grown-up

Ages 6–8

  • ☐ Make the bed
  • ☐ Set and clear the table
  • ☐ Match socks from the clean laundry
  • ☐ Wipe the bathroom sink

Ages 9–12

  • ☐ Load or unload the dishwasher
  • ☐ Vacuum one room
  • ☐ Take out trash and recycling
  • ☐ Help cook one simple meal a week

Teens

  • ☐ Do their own laundry
  • ☐ Cook dinner once a week
  • ☐ Shovel the walk or clean the car
  • ☐ Plan and shop for one family meal

A daily rhythm for the weeks off

Without the school schedule, days can blur together. A simple rhythm gives the chore chart a place in the day, so chores happen before screens instead of turning into an evening argument.

  • ☐ Morning: get dressed, make the bed, breakfast dishes to the sink
  • ☐ Before screen time: one daily chore from the chart
  • ☐ Afternoon: outside time or a quiet activity
  • ☐ Before dinner: 10-minute tidy of shared rooms
  • ☐ Evening: lay out clothes for tomorrow

Holidays, travel and visitors will interrupt some days. That is fine: keep the chart, skip the day, and pick it up again the next morning.

One bigger helper job each week

  • ☐ Week 1: help put up or take down holiday decorations
  • ☐ Week 2: sort outgrown clothes and toys to donate
  • ☐ Help write the grocery list and find items at the store
  • ☐ Clean out the car or the backpack before school starts again

The short version: four tasks per child

A long chart on the fridge is easy to stop noticing after a few days. Four tasks a day per child is short enough to see at a glance.

List “Emma’s jobs” (example, ages 6–8)

  1. Make the bed
  2. Clear the table after lunch
  3. Match socks
  4. Toys back in the bin before dinner

List “Leo’s jobs” (example, ages 9–12)

  1. Unload the dishwasher
  2. Trash and recycling out
  3. Vacuum the living room
  4. Lay out clothes for tomorrow

Frequently asked questions

When is winter break 2026?

It depends on the school district. Many U.S. schools are off for about two weeks around late December and early January; check your district's calendar for exact dates.

How many chores should a kid have over winter break?

A short list works for most families: two or three daily chores that fit the child's age, plus one bigger helper job a week.

What chores can a 6-year-old do?

Common choices are making the bed, setting and clearing the table, matching socks and putting toys away. Show the job once before adding it to the chart.

Can I make a chore chart for two kids in the free version of Mochi?
