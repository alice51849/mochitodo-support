A cute, aesthetic to-do list app you will actually open: Mochi on iPhone

If you have downloaded three to-do apps this year and stopped opening all of them, the problem was probably not discipline. Most task apps look like a spreadsheet, so the list turns into one more chore. This page shows what Mochi looks like, using only its real App Store screenshots and store listing, what the free version covers, and when the one-time Premium unlock is worth it, so you can decide in a few minutes whether it fits the way you plan school, work and errands.

What you actually see: the four App Store screenshots

Mochi’s App Store page has four iPhone screenshots. Here is what each one shows, so you know what the app looks like before you install it.

  1. “To-dos on your Home Screen.” A widget shaped like a small notepad sits on the Home Screen with a Today list: “Morning stretch” is already checked off, and “Buy fresh flowers”, “Call grandma” and “Read 10 pages” are still open. You see the day without opening the app.
  2. “Tap to check it off.” The main list has lists as tabs (Today and Work, plus a + button), a count of what is left, a progress bar and soft pink circles. Each task has its own emoji: a tulip for the flowers, a book for the reading, a plane for “Plan weekend trip”, a coffee cup for “Treat myself to a latte”.
  3. “101 cute themes.” The Skins screen lists notepad-style themes such as Mochi, Peachy, Strawberry Milk, Sunset, Watermelon and Carnival.
  4. “Cute emoji for every to-do.” Editing a task shows a note field, a Remind me switch and emoji grouped into Tasks, Home and Work & Study.

The store listing adds the rest: reminders, a daily planner and schedule, habit and routine tracking, notes and grocery lists, Home Screen widgets, and checking your list from Apple Watch.

Why “cute” is a practical feature for a to-do list

A to-do list only works if you look at it. People who search for a cute or aesthetic to-do list app are usually not asking for decoration; they are asking for a list they will not avoid. Mochi’s store listing describes the idea plainly: add tasks, tap the little circle when you finish, and enjoy the tiny burst of calm that comes from checking something off.

  • The list stays in sight. The Home Screen widget shows today’s tasks every time you unlock your phone, so the list is not hidden inside an app you forget to open.
  • Finishing feels like something. A tap on a pink circle, a crossed-out line and a progress bar that moves are small rewards, and small rewards make it easier to come back to the list every day.
  • It matches your phone. If your Home Screen is pastel, a grey task app sticks out. A theme you like is a reason to keep the widget there.
  • Your wrist works too. Mochi has an Apple Watch app, so you can check items off while your phone is in your bag.

A five-minute aesthetic setup for school, work and errands

  1. Install Mochi and make one list called Today. Keep it to four tasks: that is the free limit per list, and four is also a realistic number for a school or work day.
  2. Pick one of the five free themes that matches your wallpaper.
  3. Give each task an emoji you will recognise at a glance: a book for reading, a cart for groceries, a laptop for an assignment.
  4. Turn on Remind me for anything that has a time, such as a class deadline or a call.
  5. Add the Mochi widget to your Home Screen: touch and hold an empty spot, tap the edit or + button, search for Mochi and add the size you like. The widget guide has the details for each screen.
  6. Use the second free list for errands or groceries, and check items off on Apple Watch while you shop.

Free version or Premium: who needs what

From the store listing: the free version includes 2 lists with up to 4 tasks each, 5 themes and 1 emoji set. Premium unlocks unlimited lists and tasks, all 101 themes and all 11 emoji sets. Premium is a one-time in-app purchase, not a subscription. The price is shown in the app and on the App Store in your country.

Frequently asked questions

Is Mochi free?

Yes. The free version includes 2 lists with up to 4 tasks each, 5 themes and 1 emoji set. Premium is a one-time in-app purchase that unlocks unlimited lists and tasks, all 101 themes and all 11 emoji sets.

Does Mochi have widgets and an Apple Watch app?

Yes. The store listing says you can keep important tasks on your Home Screen with widgets and check your list from Apple Watch.

Can I use it as a planner for school?

Yes. The listing names school, work, errands, groceries, habits, routines, schedules, notes and daily goals. With the free version, keep one list for today and one for the week; with Premium you can make a list for every class or project.

What data does Mochi collect?

The privacy section of Mochi’s App Store page in the United States says “Data Not Collected”.
