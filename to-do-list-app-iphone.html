What a to-do list app needs to do (and what it does not)

Strip away the features and a to-do list does four jobs. If an app does these four well, it will work; if it misses one, you will drift back to scraps of paper.

  1. Capture fast. Adding a task should take a few seconds, or you will tell yourself you will remember it later.
  2. Stay in sight. A list you have to go looking for is a list you forget. A Home Screen widget or a watch app puts it where your eyes already are.
  3. Make finishing visible. Ticking something off should feel like progress, not like deleting a row.
  4. Remind you at the right time. Tasks with a deadline or an appointment need an alert; everything else does not.

Things a to-do list does not need for most people: project boards, tags, priorities on every item, or a team inbox. Those help teams; for school, work errands and groceries they mostly add steps between you and the checkbox.

Your iPhone already has two places for lists, and for some people they are enough.

A practical rule: if you already open Reminders every day and it works, keep it. If you have tried it and stopped opening it, the reason is usually that the list did not stay in sight or did not feel good to use, and that is exactly the gap a dedicated to-do list app is for.

A ten-minute daily method that keeps the list short

  1. Brain dump once. Write everything on your mind into one note. Do not sort it yet.
  2. Pick today’s four. Move only four tasks onto a list called Today. Four finished tasks beat twelve half-started ones, and it is a number you can actually see in a widget.
  3. Time only what has a time. Class deadline, a call at 3 pm, a pharmacy pickup: turn on a reminder for those. Leave the rest without alerts so the alerts still mean something.
  4. Keep a second list for errands. Groceries and shopping change every day; keeping them apart stops them from crowding out real work.
  5. Close the day. In the evening, tick off what is done, move one or two leftovers to tomorrow and delete what no longer matters.

If you do this for a week, the list stops being a record of everything you have not done and becomes a short plan for today.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a separate to-do list app if I have Reminders?

Not always. If you open Reminders every day and it works for you, keep it. A separate to-do list app helps when you have stopped looking at your list, for example because you want it on your Home Screen in a widget or want checking things off to feel more rewarding.

How many tasks should be on a daily to-do list?

Keep today’s list short: around four tasks that must happen today, plus a separate list for errands. Everything else can wait in a longer list or a note until you choose it for a specific day.

Is Mochi free?

Yes. The free version includes 2 lists with up to 4 tasks each, 5 themes and 1 emoji set. Premium is an in-app purchase that unlocks unlimited lists and tasks, all 101 themes and all 11 emoji sets.

Does Mochi work on Apple Watch?

Yes. The App Store listing says you can check your list from Apple Watch and keep important tasks on your Home Screen with widgets.

What data does Mochi collect?
